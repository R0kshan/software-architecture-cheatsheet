**Architecture only provides value if it meets business needs.**
## 1. Business Context: The Reason the Project Exists
- **History and Origin:** Where does the need come from? What is the client's context?
- **Added Value:** What are the real business challenges addressed by the system?
- **Success Criteria:** How will the client measure if the project is a success?

## 2. Stakeholders and Ecosystem: Problem Data
Mapping the environment :
- **Users:** List the roles, the number of current users, and the expected "growth curve" to anticipate volume (checkout [Questions to ask before defining a solution](Questions%20to%20ask%20before%20defining%20a%20solution.md))
- **External Systems:** Exhaustively list the systems (peripheral IT) that will need to interface and exchange data.
## 3. Client Need: Understand and Challenge
- **Understand and Refine:** Identify prerequisites and initial opportunities.
- **Reformulate:** This is the most critical stage. Always have the need reformulated and validated to avoid misunderstandings.
- **Challenge:** Do not take the need for granted; identify similar needs and qualify the real complexity.
## 4. Uncertainties and Risks
- **Identify uncertainties and hazards.**
- **Warning:** Not all risks can be handled by technical solutions.
- It is necessary to know how to identify those that fall under **organizational** or **business** domains.
